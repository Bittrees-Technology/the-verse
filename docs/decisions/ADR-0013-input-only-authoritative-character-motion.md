# ADR-0013: Use one atomic input-only EVA physics step

**Status:** Accepted

## Context

Before P0.9, the native client integrated EVA and provisional surface locomotion locally, then submitted an absolute `MovePlayer` position. The server bounded and collision-swept that proposal, but a modified client still chose the final displacement and supplied its own frame timing. That was not an acceptable authority boundary for mining range, combat, safe zones, logistics, or multiplayer.

The local proof already has server-owned radial gravity, voxel and grid geometry, fixed-step timing, durable control inputs for grids, canonical Jolt physics outcomes, and client-side visual prediction. Character motion should enter that same atomic physics step without pretending that the P0 sphere-cast controller is a grounded humanoid solver.

## Decision

Protocol 9 replaces `MovePlayer { position }` with `SetPlayerControl` and exposes distinct received and processed input-sequence frontiers. A character control contains a server-owned `movement_epoch`, a monotonic `input_sequence`, bounded local translation and angular vectors, boost, and dampener input. It contains no position, orientation, velocity, surface-contact result, collision result, elapsed time, or jump input. Mouse pitch/yaw and `Q`/`E` roll are angular input; the server integrates and normalizes the canonical quaternion in the character's local frame.

Content schema 7 and manifest `p0.9.0` own character mass and collision radius; thrust, boost, linear/angular damping and acceleration; normal and boost speed limits; maximum angular speed; and the control-lease length. World schema 12 stores canonical character position, orientation, linear and angular velocity, `movement_epoch`, the last received and last processed `input_sequence`, a bounded pending-control FIFO, current controls, the absolute simulation tick at which the active control expires, and the current `surface_contact` boolean. Event schema 7 persists `PlayerControlSet` under the queued-consumption semantics and extends `PhysicsStepCommitted` with one optional `PlayerPhysicsOutcome`; there is no separate player-motion system event.

The worker advances character and grid physics together in 60 Hz substeps derived only from server time. A living character is one content-sized, content-massed dynamic Jolt sphere with `LinearCast` motion quality. It occupies the same derived scene as the static spherical planet body, voxel-chunk bodies, and static or dynamic grid bodies. Each substep converts canonical local translation and angular controls into bounded world-space force and torque. Jetpack flight accelerates toward a server-owned target speed; boost and inertial dampening are server-owned rules. With the jetpack off, translation input supplies no walking force: existing velocity and gravity remain active so the character may fall and land. Jolt integrates the body's pose, linear/angular velocity, continuous collision detection, and contact response atomically with grid motion.

The character body and collider have stable Verse identifiers. `surface_contact` is a convenience boolean derived from whether the final substep's canonical active-contact set contains that player body. Stable body/collider identities, contact phase, and quantized manifold telemetry remain in the ordinary `PhysicsStepCommitted` contact outcomes. A later final substep without a player contact clears the boolean. It is contact evidence for presentation and future locomotion work, not grounded-locomotion authority. Player body outcomes are finite, globally bounded, normalized, and quantized before they enter canonical state. Live validation applies conservative per-step translation and rotation envelopes plus bounded contact penetration. Replay validates those envelopes and the prior canonical state, but intentionally does not rerun Jolt or claim to reconstruct the exact forces that produced a committed velocity.

Grid and player motion use one shared fixed-step phase and one atomic `PhysicsStepCommitted` system event. Before each substep, the server retires expired pending entries and consumes at most one surviving control transition. Its optional `PlayerPhysicsOutcome` records player identity; resulting position, orientation, linear/angular velocity, and surface-contact boolean; resulting bounded controls, boost, and dampeners; and the absolute active-control expiry tick. `PhysicsStepCommitted` records the shared step count, remaining phase, body outcomes, contact outcomes, and active-contact set. The player field is absent when no living character participates. The movement epoch, sequence frontiers, and pending FIFO remain durable canonical player state rather than being duplicated in every system outcome. Live execution uses Jolt; replay deterministically advances the FIFO, validates the committed bounds, contact identity, and prior canonical state, then applies the quantized outcome without rerunning historic Jolt solves.

Controls use an 18-tick server-step lease, equal to 300 milliseconds at the pinned 60 Hz step. `PlayerControlSet` records the absolute expiry tick computed at durable receipt. Acceptance advances `last_received_input_sequence` and appends to a persisted FIFO bounded by the lease horizon; it does not advance the processed acknowledgement or immediately overwrite the active control. Each fixed substep retires expired entries without motion, otherwise consumes one transition and advances `last_processed_input_sequence`. A press and its following release therefore survive scheduler phase and are observed on successive substeps. A fresh, in-order control intent receives its own lease even when the directional values are unchanged. Operation retries are idempotent; an input sequence is monotonic against the received frontier within its movement epoch, and another epoch or an old sequence cannot revive stale control. When the active lease expires, translation, angular input, and boost clear and dampeners engage. Disconnects and packet loss therefore cannot leave a character accelerating or rotating indefinitely.

An accepted `PlayerControlSet` is appended before its received frontier or pending FIFO is published. A failure before the journal write leaves the prior queue durable; a failure after synchronization recovers the complete queued transition. For a live physics step, the worker stages and validates the complete grid and optional player outcome plus deterministic FIFO advancement before appending the single `PhysicsStepCommitted` event. Failure before durability halts the runtime without publishing the staged outcome; restart rebuilds the derived scene from the unchanged durable canonical state. Failure after journal synchronization also halts the runtime, and restart recovers the committed event before rebuilding Jolt from that durable state. Neither boundary can publish a partial control, player-only, or grid-only step.

Incapacitation clears linear/angular velocity, active and pending motion input, boost, surface contact, and the control lease atomically. Respawn advances the server-owned movement epoch, resets both input-sequence frontiers, clears the FIFO, and starts from the server-selected point with identity orientation, zero velocity, and dampeners enabled. Dead players cannot submit controls. A `PhysicsStepCommitted` event contains no player outcome for an incapacitated player.

The native client may predict the same movement for responsiveness. It samples in its fixed physics loop, allocates after the server's received frontier, keeps bounded pending and prediction-history buffers, resets simulation to each newer canonical player state, discards inputs through the processed frontier, and replays only unprocessed frames. Presentation snaps large, reconnect, history-gap, or lifecycle/epoch errors and smooths bounded small corrections. Prediction cannot extend interaction range or suppress authoritative collision. Reconnect starts from the latest canonical pose and velocity, then allocates after `last_received_input_sequence` so already durable pending controls cannot collide with reused sequence numbers.

Motion replication uses a lightweight versioned player-state message at a bounded cadence. Full world snapshots remain for handshake, world changes, and recovery rather than causing every character tick to rebuild every grid and voxel presentation.

## Consequences

- A client can choose bounded translation and angular intent but cannot choose character position, orientation, velocity, collision, gravity, or elapsed time.
- Mining, welding, construction, damage, and inventory range checks use a position produced by the server controller.
- Control and motion events increase local P0 journal and replication traffic. P0 provides only a bounded per-player control FIFO; P1 authenticated session ownership, spatial interest management, aggregate backpressure, and production binary deltas remain required before large-scale multiplayer.
- The bounded sphere-cast controller is clean-room Verse code and does not reproduce Space Engineers implementation details.
- Historic replay applies committed outcomes rather than depending on cross-platform reproduction of Jolt floating-point queries. Live macOS and Linux results still require quantization after every substep and cross-platform tolerance evidence.

## Required evidence

- Protocol serialization proves that character-control messages contain no pose, orientation, velocity, collision, or client delta-time fields.
- Invalid, non-finite, or over-limit controls reject before mutation.
- Equivalent input and server time produce the exact expected EVA, boost, drift, dampening, gravity, landing, and local-frame rotation outcomes.
- The dynamic Jolt sphere's `LinearCast` motion quality prevents tunneling through the static planet, a voxel, or a grid while nearby clear motion remains possible.
- Landing produces the expected canonical contact pair and surface-contact boolean without enabling walking or jump behavior.
- Stale controls expire into a safe dampened state, including after disconnect and restart.
- Replay rejects a mismatched prior canonical state, out-of-envelope pose or rotation, non-finite or globally out-of-bound velocity, invalid timing/contact phase, wrong movement epoch or sequence frontier, forged surface contact or collision identity, and noncanonical lease/control fields before mutation.
- Control retries are idempotent; queued press/release transitions survive scheduler phase; queue backpressure rejects without mutation; and before-write and after-sync control or motion failures recover exactly the complete prior or durable outcome.
- Incapacitation and respawn clear motion state exactly and survive journal and snapshot restart.
- Lightweight player-state updates distinguish durable receipt from fixed-step processing without triggering full world rebuilds; native impairment coverage exercises acknowledgement replay, correction smoothing/snaps, menu-open gravity, bounded history, skipped or stale updates, and dead/disconnected gating.
- The cross-process scenario moves using input only, performs a range-gated voxel action, restarts, and recovers the exact canonical position, velocity, controls, and world hash.

## Deliberate limits

P0.9 does not add walking, jump, or a canonical grounded-locomotion state. Those capabilities, plus slopes, stairs, ladders, magnetic boots, and moving-platform attachment, are explicitly P0.10-or-later work. Crouching, ragdolls, character-to-character collision, suit fuel or power consumption, impact damage, animation-driven motion, lag compensation, rollback networking, authenticated player ownership, and multiplayer interest management also remain later. P0.9 does not claim production delayed/permissioned browser spectating. Grid cockpits and ship-control possession remain separate work.
